 What is Telemonitoring and How Does it Help Patients?
Site Author
 Andrew Molnar
Abstract
 This site explains what telemonitoring is and how it has benefited patients thus far. Telemonitoring is a form of remote patient monitoring that allows healthcare providers to track their patient's health while being at home. It involves the use of glucose monitors, pulse oximeters, and other tools with a Bluetooth connection that can send health information directly to the patient's healthcare team. It has been extremely useful for patients with chronic conditions, including heart failure, as it allows providers to stop a complication before it even occurs or escalates. Telemonitoring helps improve patient outcomes, reduce healthcare costs, and even decrease readmission rates. In fact, this site explains how telemonitoring helped a monitoring program reach a 0% heart failure readmission rate for two months. With telemonitoring in place, providers can help patients manage their conditions from home.

 Using Computers in Schools
Site Author
 Eric Rusten
Abstract
 This site is actually a journal article written to address what can be done to utilize computers properly in education.  It especially focuses on bridging the information gap in less developed countries, such as Brazil.  Rusten explains that when computers are used effectively, they can be extremely helpful.  The can improve teaching and learning and strengthen school managment.  He describes four plans that he feels will enable computers and technology to reach the classroom.  They are Computer Assisted Learning (CAL), Simulation and Exploration, Research and Analysis, and Computation and Production.
